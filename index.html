The World Ends With You (TWEWY) was a DS JRPG released around 2007-2008 and developed by the team at Square responsible for a tiny little franchise you've probably never heard of it, called Kingdom Hearts. The game was about an emo teen named Neku who found himself and others wandering Shibuya, Japan, as ghosts. They are told by mysterious humans with black wings, called Reapers, that they have met with an untimely demise, and if they want a second chance at life, they must survive for 7-Days in what's called The Reapers Game. Completing challenges the Reapers send them and defeating waves of creatures called Noise, negative emotions that attach themselves to people in the living world. (They're basically the Heartless or Unversed from Kingdom Hearts). If they succeeded, some, but not all, would be given the chance to come back to life; anyone else who survived the 7-days would be given the choice to play again, join the Reapers, or quit and face the sweet release of oblivion like anyone who died to the Noise. That's the basic set-up for the game's plot. Before I move on, obviously there are a bunch of twists and turns, but I'm not really going to discuss that because, one, it's spoilers and you should totally play this game if you like Kingdom Hearts. (Seriosuly its really good and probably the best game on there to be almost pure stylus controls.) And two, I want to analyze the Reapers as an organization, what their purpose and job are, the structure and rules it has to follow, and how certain individuals in it behave and act. Getting into the game's actual plot opens up a whole can of moral worms that I personally don't believe are relevant to the Reapers' morality as a whole, and because the game itself says that this specific Reapers Game is a very, very special case. So without further ado, what is the morality of it being your job to hunt down people who have just died? People who don't even know what's going on, and are just trying to win whatever crazy game this is, just for the small chance at getting another shot at life? The Reapers are a world-spanning organization made up of people who died and survived the Reapers Game, but either didn't win the ultimate prize, a second chance at life, or willingly chose to become a Shinigami-like figure so they could still somewhat *live*, staving off their oblivion. Basic Reaper structure is as follows; presumably, each town, city, district, or small country has its own Branch of Reapers that plays its own Reapers Game with the local dead. These Branches all act independently of each other and can have their own rules for how the Game is played, but still have to follow certain universal rules, or else face oblivion. Each Branch is ruled over by a near godlike Reaper called The Composer; they set the main rules for how their local Reaper's Game is played, and ultimately decide the conditions by which Players get their second chance at life, should they even win. They are immensely powerful and can control the entire composition and existence of their local cluster of Purgatory. Beside them is the Conductor, their second in command, and the one other Reaper most Composers interact with. Their job is to interpret and execute the Composers' rules, appoint Game Masters and Officers, and perform the final review on Players that survived the 7-day Reaper's Game. The Game Master is a high-performing Reaper chosen by The Conductor to be the Big Bad Boss of the Reaper's Game for the Players. They design and set the rules for the daily missions the Players must complete and command the Rank and File. At the end of the 7th Day, the GM can directly attack and kill any Player. If he is defeated, the Players win the Reaper's Game. There are also Officers, other high-ranking Reapers, or previous week's GMs that sit around and talk in the background, chill out, or come to poke fun at you. Then there are the Harriers, the most active members of the Reapers. It's their job to actively hunt Players over the week; however, under the game's standard rules, they are forbidden from attacking Players directly; instead, they summon The Noise (this game's Heartless or Unversed). They can, however, act in self-defense and directly fight back if a Player is attempting to kill them. And last, there are the Support Reapers. These shmucks go into the living world to buy supplies, guard barriers in the Game, and issue minor missions. Well, not last, because we have the Players. People who have died end up in a ghostly afterlife version of their hometown or whatever; the Reapers tell them they can win a chance at life by surviving a 7-Day Reapers Game. And in order to play the Reapers Game, you have to give up what you treasured most in life. The Players are then split up into groups of two and tasked with completing a mission on each day; however, these missions have time limits. If the Players fail to complete the mission in time, they are fully dead and sent to oblivion. (narrative time limit only, not in gameplay, thank god) During that time, though, they will be hunted by Harriers and Noise, as well as having to complete smaller missions to unlock barriers erected around the Game. Players are given items that unlock psychic powers (pins for Shibuya) that allow them to defend themselves from Noise. Players are also allowed to briefly phase into existence when entering stores, so they can purchase food and supplies. It's not all bad for the Players, as only one Team has to complete the main daily mission for everyone else to live for that day, and points are earned not necessarily by completing the main objective anyway. Players, despite competing to come back to life and being separated into teams, are encouraged to work together with other teams. Completing other objectives and watching each other's backs. On the 7th day, the players who survived that long are directly challenged by that week's Game Master, who can now directly attack players. Once the GM is defeated, the Conductor comes out and congratulates the surviving Players and begins his evaluation. It is they who decide which of them will return to life, get to play the game again, join the Reapers, or face oblivion, based on the Rules and Conditions laid out to him by The Composer. (Remember, all of which could be different for each Branch around the globe)
